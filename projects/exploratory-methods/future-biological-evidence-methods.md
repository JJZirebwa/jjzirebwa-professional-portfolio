# Future biological evidence methods

## Summary

This page summarises exploratory thinking about future biological evidence methods for rare-disease and underpowered research settings. It is included as bounded methods thinking.

## Context

Rare-disease research often has small samples, incomplete clinical histories, missing biological measurements and difficult validation conditions. My final-year project made those limits concrete and raised a practical question: how can future studies decide what evidence to collect next without inventing unsupported evidence?

## Method themes

- provenance-aware biological priors
- hypothesis-generation support
- study-design support
- missing-variable discovery
- disproof-first validation
- anti-circularity controls
- negative controls and shuffled-prior comparisons
- ablation testing
- clear separation between observed evidence, derived features and augmented assumptions

## What this could support

At an exploratory level, this thinking could support:

- identifying features worth measuring in a future study
- testing whether biological assumptions add structure beyond random baselines
- prioritising mechanistic pathways for follow-up
- designing validation plans before expensive data collection
- making evidence gaps explicit
