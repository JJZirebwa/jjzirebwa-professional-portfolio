# Future biological evidence methods

## Summary

This page summarises exploratory thinking about weak-signal biological evidence methods for rare-disease and underpowered research settings. It is concept-stage methods thinking, not a validated platform or clinical tool.

## Context

Rare-disease research often has small samples, incomplete clinical histories, missing biological measurements and difficult validation conditions. My final-year project made those limits concrete and raised a practical question: how can future studies prioritise the next evidence decision without inventing unsupported evidence?

## Method themes

- recovering useful signal without pretending sparse data is richer than it is
- using biological context to prioritise what to measure next
- generating sharper hypotheses and follow-up study designs
- stress-testing assumptions with negative controls, shuffled-prior comparisons and ablations
- keeping observed evidence, derived features and augmented assumptions clearly separate

## What this could support

At an exploratory level, this thinking could support:

- identifying features worth measuring in a future study
- testing whether biological assumptions add structure beyond random baselines
- prioritising mechanistic pathways for follow-up
- designing validation plans before expensive data collection
- making evidence gaps explicit
