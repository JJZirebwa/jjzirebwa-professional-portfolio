# Biomedical Science degree

## Overview

**Degree:** BSc Biomedical Science, Anglia Ruskin University, Cambridge  
**Status:** Completed; First Class Honours awarded on 10 June 2026  
**Placement year:** Health Innovation East commercial placement, 2024-2025

My degree has given me a broad biomedical science base across microbiology, anatomy, physiology, cell biology, biochemistry, pathology, genetics, metabolism, pharmacology and clinical science. The later stages of the course connect laboratory and mechanistic science to evidence interpretation, translational medicine and health-data research.

## Academic direction

My academic profile is strongest where biomedical science must be interpreted through data, clinical context and decision-making:

- understanding disease mechanisms and how they appear in clinical or laboratory evidence
- interpreting genetics and phenotype relationships
- reading biomedical literature and converting it into structured argument
- handling quantitative evidence, from genetic tests to pharmacology dose-response curves
- thinking about how biomedical evidence informs healthcare adoption, risk and governance

## Practical laboratory work

My laboratory experience comes from supervised and assessed degree practicals. It is degree-based experience rather than professional laboratory employment, and I keep simulated or computational exercises separate from the hands-on methods below.

### Microbiology

General Microbiology included aseptic technique, culture handling, agar preparation, streak and spread plating, Gram staining, oxidase and catalase testing, disk diffusion, colony counting and CFU/ml calculations. The assessed practical series used a mock food-poisoning investigation, so the individual tests had to be read together before identifying the unknown organism.

### DNA manipulation and protein expression

Laboratory Techniques in DNA Manipulation covered micropipetting, solution preparation, PCR, agarose gel electrophoresis, spin-column purification, restriction digestion, ligation, plating, colony PCR, clone-screening logic and SDS-PAGE interpretation. The module included an observed practical assessment covering pipetting, gel loading, weighing and molar-solution preparation.

### Diagnostic pathology

Diagnostic Techniques in Pathology included specimen-reception and LIMS exercises, microbiology practicals, indirect ELISA with a standard curve and plate-reader measurement, and H&E and Papanicolaou staining. The work introduced controls, SOP-led practice, result interpretation and quality-system concepts. I describe specimen reception and LIMS as workflow exercises, not routine clinical laboratory practice.

### Microscopy and biochemistry

Human Anatomy and Physiology and Introduction to Cell Biology and Biochemistry included light microscopy across several magnifications, graticule calibration, oil immersion, tissue observation, enzyme assays and protein purification. These were practical teaching and assessment settings. They do not support claims of independent instrument maintenance, fault diagnosis or advanced-analyser operation.
